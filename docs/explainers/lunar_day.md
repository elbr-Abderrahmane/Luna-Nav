# The lunar day

One full cycle of day and night on the Moon takes about **29.53 Earth days** (a synodic month): roughly two weeks of daylight followed by two weeks of night at most places.

That is very different from Earth's 24 hours. A lander that arrives at sunrise has about two weeks to work before sunset, and then must survive about two weeks of cold darkness.

At the south pole the picture is more complex: the Sun circles the horizon, so some ridges stay lit for most of the cycle while nearby craters stay in permanent shadow.

LunaNav analyses one lunar day (29.53 days) by default starting from the chosen landing date. Moving the landing date by one month can change the result completely, because the Sun's angle changes through the year.
