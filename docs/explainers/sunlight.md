# Why sunlight matters

A lander on the Moon runs on solar panels. No Sun means no power: no radio, no instruments and no heaters. The lunar night is also extremely cold, so batteries must keep the electronics alive until the Sun returns.

LunaNav uses two numbers:

- **Sunlight (%)**: the share of the analysis window during which the centre of the Sun is above the local horizon.
- **Longest night (h)**: the longest uninterrupted period without Sun. This is what a battery and heater system must survive.

A site can have lots of sunlight overall and still have one very long night. That is why we look at both numbers.

**Near the poles** the Sun never climbs high: it skims the horizon, so panels are often mounted vertically. A hill on the horizon can hide the Sun for days, which is why small changes of location or date have large effects.

*Power in LunaNav is geometry only: no panel efficiency, temperature or dust.*
