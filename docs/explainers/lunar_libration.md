# Lunar libration

The Moon always shows nearly the same face to Earth, but it seems to **wobble** a little. This is called libration. It comes from the Moon's slightly oval orbit and the tilt of its axis, and it moves Earth up and down by a few degrees in a lander's sky over the month.

Why it matters: near the edge of the visible disc, a few degrees decide whether Earth is above or below the horizon. Earth can slip in and out of view several times, creating the communication gaps shown by LunaNav.

At the south pole Earth sits very low in the sky, so libration (together with hills on the horizon) controls when direct communication is possible. LunaNav includes libration in its Earth positions and reports how far Earth wobbles in the sky for each scenario.
